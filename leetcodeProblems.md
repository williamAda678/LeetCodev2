## Problem-Solving Process

For every problem, follow this sequence:

1. Understand the problem and restate it in your own words.
2. Try a brute-force solution first.
3. Analyze the time and space complexity.
4. Identify the pattern involved.
5. Implement the solution.
6. Explain the approach clearly, as if in an interview.

Recommended Time Limits

Don't spend hours completely stuck.

Easy 15–20 minutes
Medium 25–35 minutes
Hard 40–45 minutes

If you're stuck:

Try to identify the pattern.

Look for a small hint.

Try again.

Only then look at the solution.

Close the solution.

Implement it yourself.

Redo it later without assistance.

Review Schedule

Don't solve a problem once and forget about it.

Review difficult problems:

1st attempt: Day 1

2nd attempt: 2–3 days later

3rd attempt: 1–2 weeks later

4th attempt: Before an interview

The goal is pattern recognition, not memorization.

C# Interview Toolkit

Become comfortable with:

int[] nums;

List<int> list;

Dictionary<int, int> map;

HashSet<int> set;

Stack<int> stack;

Queue<int> queue;

PriorityQueue<int, int> heap;

Also know:

Array.Sort(nums);

list.Sort();

map.ContainsKey(key);

map.TryGetValue(key, out var value);

set.Contains(value);

queue.Enqueue(value);
queue.Dequeue();

stack.Push(value);
stack.Pop();

And understand the typical time complexity of common operations.

12-Week Goal

By the end of the roadmap, don't measure success by the number of problems completed.

Measure whether you can see a new problem and think:

"This looks like a sliding-window problem."

"This probably needs a HashSet."

"This is a BFS problem."

"This looks like binary search."

"I need a heap here."

"This is probably dynamic programming."

The goal is to develop pattern recognition + problem solving + clear communication.

That is much more valuable than simply completing hundreds of problems.
